### An update of orders fails 28% of the time (1205 lock wait timeout)

**28% of runs fail** · Needs attention · high impact, high confidence · MySQL 8.4.11, local environment

The associated statements used 12.0 s of database time over the 2 minutes since statistics were reset (4%). 12 of its 43 runs since its statistics began 2 minutes ago didn't finish. 12 ended in an error; the recent ones were 1205 lock wait timeout. A run that doesn't finish still costs the time it ran, and the application retries it or shows an error.

- Runs: **43**
- Didn't finish: **28%**
- Errors: **12**
- Recent error: **1205 lock wait timeout**

**Query `4515abd524a46f37`** · 4% of database time · 43 runs over the 2 minutes since statistics were reset · 280 ms on average

```sql
UPDATE `orders` SET STATUS = STATUS WHERE `id` = ?
```

**Fix.** Another transaction holds the rows it needs for longer than innodb\_lock\_wait\_timeout. Find what holds the locks while it waits (Live shows the blocking chain). Keep transactions short. The recent errors and their messages are in performance\_schema.

```sql
SELECT MYSQL_ERRNO, RETURNED_SQLSTATE, MESSAGE_TEXT
  FROM performance_schema.events_statements_history
 WHERE DIGEST = '4515abd524a46f378b048f32e532cb1befdfd6c88178caeab12746b1f6c2b9c6' AND MYSQL_ERRNO <> 0;
```

**How to verify**

1. Between two scans, runs that fail or are cancelled should drop to near zero: performance_schema keeps running totals, so compare its failed runs and runs in a scan taken once the change is live and one a day or more later (12 of 43 runs in this scan).

**Before you act**

- MySQL counts errors from when each statement's entry began counting, so old failures stay in its total until the counters are reset or the server restarts.
